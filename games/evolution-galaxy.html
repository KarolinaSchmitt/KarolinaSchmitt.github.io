Evolution Galaxy: Mutant Merge

Mobile · Merge / idle / collection

Evolution Galaxy: Mutant Merge is a free-to-play merge and idle game that takes the evolution series into space. Players drag and combine creatures to evolve new mutants, then use their eggs and quick taps to earn the coins that unlock more species. Each planet brings its own theme, gods to discover and impostors, all in a hand-drawn doodle style.

I joined Evolution Galaxy when it was already live, as the only game designer in a five-person team. As an older game, its main fight was against the natural drop in store relevance.

The team's goal was to maximize the game's metrics with as few resources as possible, running data-driven A/B tests and moving monetization from ads to in-app purchases (IAP).

My contributions

  • A/B testingI ran data-driven tests to optimize the IAP offers, covering price, composition, frequency and timing of display.
  • Monetization designI took part in the transition from an ads-based model to in-app purchases (IAP), rebalancing the IAP store and creating new offers.
  • Economy & balancingI balanced the game's economy: soft and hard currency values, progression speed and power-ups.
  • LiveOps designI designed new LiveOps events.
  • Promotional contentI created a promotional content system designed to be optimized and reused across campaigns.
  • Balancing spreadsheetsI maintained the game's balancing spreadsheets.

Challenges & solutions

Keeping an aging game relevant with limited resources

Store visibility falls naturally over time, and a small team has little room for major overhauls. The answer was to prioritize changes with high impact and low cost, and to build systems that could be optimized and reused across campaigns instead of rebuilt each time.

Moving monetization from ads to in-app purchases

Changing how a game makes money affects the whole experience. Growing IAP revenue meant treating the store, the offers and the economy as one system, and replacing intuition with testing: the price, composition, frequency and timing of display of each offer were all tested.

Deciding with data when resources are limited

With a small team, every change had to justify its cost. Decisions followed a cycle: compare versions in A/B tests, read the metrics of each one, and turn what worked into a system that could be applied again.

Results

Despite the natural decline in relevance of an older game, Evolution Galaxy's metrics stayed stable during the period of work on the project.

  • RevenueTotal revenue grew 20% in the period following the launch of a specific IAP offer.
  • A new monetization mixIAP's share of revenue doubled, from about 10% to about 20%, as monetization moved from ads to purchases.
  • Systems that scaleThe promotional content system was reused in many campaigns without having to be rebuilt.
  • Decisions backed by dataTests on price, composition, frequency and timing of display guided how the offers evolved.

In a mature game, relevance is earned with small, measured steps and tools that can be reused.
