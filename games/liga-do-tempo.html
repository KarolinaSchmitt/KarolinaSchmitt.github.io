Liga do Tempo

PlayTable & PC · Card game

Liga do Tempo is a history card game for ages 9 and up, played on the PlayTable, Playmove's interactive table, and on PC. Up to four time travelers fix a broken timeline by placing historical events in the right order, competing against each other or the machine, or cooperating in pairs. The content follows the periods indicated in the school curriculum for each age group.

Challenges & solutions

Teaching history through play

An educational game has to be accurate and enjoyable at the same time. The answer was to anchor the content in the school curriculum for each age group, with every topic verified by pedagogues and history teachers, and to carry it through a timeline mechanic that players learn by playing.

Calibrating difficulty for each age group

Players of different ages bring different levels of historical knowledge, so difficulty could not be decided on paper. It was calibrated in playtests, varying settings such as the number of cards and the information available for the player to consult.

Validating quickly with a small team

With a team of three, rules had to be validated fast. Paper prototypes needed few materials, were quick to build and made players' behavior visible in practice, showing which rules worked and allowing them to be changed on the spot.

Results

Liga do Tempo performed well. It was not expected to outsell the platform's top titles, given its slightly older age group, but it held up and filled a curriculum gap that the PlayTable did not yet cover.

  • Learning beyond the gameIn playtests with children, who had less historical knowledge, participants became interested in the topics they met in a match and researched them on their own afterward, validating the hypothesis of learning beyond the game.
  • History and cooperation togetherIn cooperative mode, children gathered to find the right place of each event on the timeline, exercising both historical learning and cooperation.
  • A curriculum gap filledThe game addressed a curriculum area that the PlayTable had not yet covered.
  • Still availableThe game remains available today, with plans to expand it to new themes.

In an educational game, the best sign of learning was curiosity that went beyond the match.
