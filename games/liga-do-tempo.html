Liga do Tempo

PlayTable & PC · Card game

Liga do Tempo is a digital history card game for ages 9 and up, on the PlayTable, Playmove's interactive table. Up to four time travelers fix a broken timeline by placing historical events in the right order, competing against each other or the machine, or cooperating in pairs. The content follows the periods indicated in the school curriculum for the age group.

I took part in every stage of Liga do Tempo, from concept to post-launch, as the only game designer in a team of three. The game is still available today.

My contributions

  • Game designI defined the whole game: concept, theme, rules and mechanics, built around placing historical events in the right order on a timeline, in turn-based play.
  • Play modesI designed the ways to play: competing against other players, competing against the machine, or cooperating in pairs, for up to four people around the same table.
  • Difficulty designI defined the levers that set the difficulty (the number of cards and the information available for the player to consult) and introduced false events as an extra layer.
  • Content designI structured the content around the school curriculum of each age group, and every topic was verified by pedagogues and history teachers.
  • PrototypingI used paper prototypes to test rule ideas quickly and at low cost, adjusting rules on the spot.
  • PlaytestingI used playtests to calibrate the rules and the difficulty, first with the development team and later with children of the age group.
  • Post-launchI took part in the plans to expand the game with new themes.

Challenges & solutions

One mechanic for competition and for cooperation

The same timeline mechanic had to work against other players, against the machine and in cooperative pairs, with up to four people at one table. The decision was a turn-based structure that keeps the game readable for everyone and gives each player autonomy over their decisions, and a cooperative mode in which the pair shares a single decision: where each event belongs on the timeline.

Difficulty as adjustable variables and extra layers

Players of different ages bring different levels of knowledge, so a single fixed difficulty would not work. Difficulty was designed as a combination of variables, the number of cards and the information available for the player to consult, and each combination was calibrated in testing. A decision that enriched the project was the addition of false events: a false event breaks the whole timeline, so in the hardest mode it rewards the most attentive players and adds a layer of complexity to the learning the game was meant to generate.

Content as a design constraint

The cards carry the game's content, and the school curriculum of each age group defined which periods could be used. Turning that curriculum into game content, with every topic verified by pedagogues and history teachers, set clear limits for the design from the start.

Deciding quickly with a small team

With a team of three, design decisions needed fast validation. Paper prototypes took few materials and were quick to build, which made it possible to see which rules worked in practice and to change them on the spot.

Results

Liga do Tempo performed well. It was not expected to outsell the platform's top titles, given its slightly older age group, but it held up and filled a curriculum gap that the PlayTable did not yet cover.

  • Cooperation as designedThe cooperative mode was designed to bring players together, using logical reasoning, to beat the machine, and it worked: children gathered to find the right place of each event on the timeline.
  • A hypothesis validatedThe design hypothesis of learning beyond the game held up: after matches, participants researched on their own the topics that interested them.
  • A gap filled in the catalogThe game addressed a curriculum area that the PlayTable had not yet covered.
  • Still availableThe game remains available today, with plans to expand it to new themes.

In this game, every rule was a design decision, tested until it served the player.
