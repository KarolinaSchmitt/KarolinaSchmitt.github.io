Divertron

PlayTable · Classic board game collection

Divertron is a collection of classic board games in digital versions for ages 5 and up, on the PlayTable, Playmove's interactive table. It was created to bring traditional games to the digital environment in schools. Players can face each other or the machine, at different difficulty levels.

Features & systems

Game modes

Divertron brings together different games, each with its own way of playing: Checkers, Reversi and Tic-Tac-Toe for two players, and Ludo for up to four. Offering a variety of classics on the same platform serves different moments and different groups: A quick match of Tic-Tac-Toe, a longer strategy game of Checkers or Reversi, or a social game of Ludo for the whole table. Each game keeps the rules players already know, so the learning curve is low, while the digital version adds what the table does well: Timers, automatic rules, scores and an opponent with difficulty levels.

Achievements

Each game has its own achievements, which reward different ways of playing and encourage the player to explore. Achievements give players goals beyond winning, from playful challenges to mastery goals; they reward curiosity and skill; and they encourage replay, since there is always something left to unlock. In a collection of classic games, they also give each one a layer of its own, so a game the player already knows still has something new to offer.

Ranking

The ranking shows the players' points and times, with a filter by game. It gives the player a way to measure progress and a reason to try again: Seeing your own name rise, or a name to overtake, turns each match into a step toward a goal. In a school or any shared space, it also creates friendly competition and a sense of community around the game, and it extends the life of each game.

Challenges & solutions

Choosing which classics to bring to the table

Not every classic board game works well on an interactive table. The criteria were fit with the digital, multiplayer proposal of the platform and the possibility of building a good AI for the game. That led to a collection with games for two players and one, Ludo, for up to four.

An opponent that plays well and plays at the right level

For the machine to be a fair opponent, its behavior was built on logical rules: Priorities and advantageous moves it evaluates on each turn. On top of that base, the game offers different difficulty levels, so the same game serves players of different experience.

Validating difficulty for the audience

With players from age 5, a difficulty that is too high is as much a problem as one that is too low. The AIs went through several rounds of playtests specifically to check that each level was right for the audience, and were adjusted until it was.

Results

Divertron was a sales success, both in schools and in the entertainment segment. It remains available on the PlayTable today.

In this game, familiar rules and a well-tuned opponent were what made the classics work on a digital table.
