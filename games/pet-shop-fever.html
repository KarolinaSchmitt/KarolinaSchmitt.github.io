Pet Shop Fever

Mobile · Time management

Pet Shop Fever is a free-to-play time-management game. Players run busy pet shops, bathing, grooming and feeding animals against the clock, then move on to new themed pet shops with new guests, rare breeds and more than 800 levels. Regular limited-time events, card collections and minigames keep it growing.

I joined Pet Shop Fever during its soft launch, as one of two game designers in an 11-person team. As the game grew into a live service, the work shifted from building the core to keeping it growing: new content, events and features, without breaking what already works.

The team's goals changed over time, and usually meant increasing retention, reducing churn, growing monetization and shipping content updates.

  • Playtesting & A/B testingI validated changes with playtesting, working in Unity and shipping with Scrum.

Challenges & solutions

Early-funnel churn

Challenge
Our early funnel already had a problem: players weren't staying for many levels. The game felt slow. In a time-management game, players should be chaining quick actions, but the character moved so slowly that they spent long stretches without acting, just waiting for it to walk from one side of the shop to the other.
Solution
We made one targeted change: we increased the character's movement speed, and nothing else. We then compared the Google Analytics data from the periods before and after the change.
Impact
Churn in the early funnel dropped by around 30%.
Learning
It taught us that players can handle more pressure, and that this helps them stay in the game.
