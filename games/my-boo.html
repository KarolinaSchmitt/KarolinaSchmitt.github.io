My Boo

Mobile · Virtual pet / simulation

My Boo is a free-to-play virtual pet game in which players care for their Boo every day and play adventure minigames to earn coins. In the store, coins unlock outfits and accessories to customize the Boo, who can even dress up as a kitten, a dog and other animals.

I joined My Boo when it was already live, as the only game designer in a five-person team. With more than ten years in the stores, the main fight was against the natural depreciation that games face over time.

The team's goal was to counter that decline with few resources, through special LiveOps events, tests and the economy behind them.

My contributions

  • Feature designI planned a collectible sticker album, in which players earn stickers by engaging with the game and with seasonal events, aiming to improve retention, mainly D30.
  • Economy & balancingFollowing the changes to the LiveOps events, I took a holistic look at the game's economy balance, adjusting it as a whole rather than event by event.
  • LiveOps designI planned a calendar of special LiveOps events, organizing them throughout the year to keep players engaged and connecting them to the economy.
  • Monetization designI ran tests with ads and planned events designed to encourage use of the store, working monetization together with the LiveOps calendar and the economy, with attention to the legal constraints of a children's audience.
  • A/B testingI ran tests on the events calendar, evaluating how to organize it so that LiveOps decisions were backed by data and the game's results were sustained.
  • Promotional contentI produced promotional content on the same tooling base as Evolution Galaxy, an easy-to-optimize and reusable system that allowed the structure to be reused in new campaigns.

Features & systems

Store and bundles

The store and its bundles follow a simple monetization principle: Offer the right thing at the right moment. Coin packs with extra free coins reward larger purchases, and offers such as ad removal and a Starter Bundle gather what a new player needs to get going into a single, time-limited package. When an offer matches a real need of the player, the purchase reads as help and not as pressure, which supports revenue while keeping the economy healthy.

Care features

Care is the heart of a virtual pet game, and in My Boo it is organized in four areas: Play, food, hygiene and health. The unlocking of care items means the player always has something more to discover while playing. It gives routine actions a sense of novelty, rewards progress with new ways to care for the Boo, and gives the player a reason to keep coming back.

Customization

Customization lets the player leave their Boo exactly the way they want, with hats, hair, clothes, colors, costumes and decoration for the room. In care games, its benefits go beyond looks: It creates emotional attachment, because the Boo becomes a reflection of the player; it gives expression and creativity a place in the game; and it creates a long-term use for the coins the player earns, which ties customization to the economy and to progression.

Minigames and achievements

Minigames and achievements give the player other ways to play, and the minigames are the source of the coins needed to buy customizations. A tied economy, in which every source of currency has a clear use and every use has a clear source, is essential for the health of a game: It keeps progression feeling fair, avoids inflation and dead ends, and makes each activity matter to the others. Here, playing minigames feeds customization, which feeds attachment and the desire to keep playing.

Progression

Every activity in the game makes the Boo gain levels. Levels matter because they unlock rewards and new features, which keeps the player engaged for a long time: There is always a next level to reach and something new waiting on the way. By tying every action to the same progression, the game makes every moment of play count.

Challenges & solutions

Driving growth in a game with more than ten years

In a long-running game, store visibility falls naturally over time, and a small team has little room for major overhauls. The answer was to give the game a predictable rhythm of novelties: A calendar of special events and a sticker album, earned through play and events, that gives players a long-term goal and targets D30 retention.

Monetizing in a game for children

Because My Boo is aimed at a children's audience, monetization and gacha-style features call for extra care to stay clear of legal issues. Planning the offers, the store-driven events and the collection mechanics meant taking those limits into account from the start.

Making events, economy and store work together

An event affects the economy and the store at the same time, and adjusting one part in isolation unbalances the others. That is why the economy was reviewed holistically whenever the events changed, rather than event by event.

Deciding with limited resources

With a small team, each change had to be validated before being kept. Tests on ads and on how the events calendar was organized guided those decisions.

Results
